### Would the results change under independent re-annotation?

They might change, but re-annotation would not address the underlying problem, which is how the dataset was collected. Votes were solicited until the first disagreement, so 47% of non-tie triplets carry three or four, and the release reports aggregated counts without rater identifiers, so individual bias cannot be separated from perceptual disagreement. About 80,000 of the 100,000 triplets are not unanimous, and split-half testing shows that 80% still disagrees heavily: reproduction runs 100% on unanimous triplets, 80% on 4-1, 50% on 3-1 and 33% on 2-1, averaging 70.1%. Fig1_rebuttal shows one triplet per pattern.

A different pool, different instructions or fatigue would add variance on top, so 70.1% is an upper bound and reproduction well above it would overturn the paper. Cohen's κ in Appendix C corroborates this without the bootstrap, declining on weak-majority triplets as the floor predicts.

The study we propose collects 3,000 triplets stratified by agreement pattern, with 30 annotators each and no early stopping, and it records per-rater votes so that individual bias can be separated. A second condition has the same annotators judge the Section 4.6 manipulations, supplying the human baseline those results currently lack, and the 608 stimuli shared with NIGHTS-JND.


### Fitting NIGHTS label noise versus learning a real but sparse signal

Of DreamSim's 6.2 pp over an untrained ensemble, 0.4 pp comes from the ensemble architecture and 2.0 pp from train/test overlap on the 20k the checkpoint trained on, which is label fitting by definition. The remaining 4.2 pp survives every control we applied.

Raising head capacity on a frozen DINOv3 CLS lowers accuracy, from 72.01% for cosine to 68.39% for an 800k-parameter head across ten heads (Appendix D.1), so the extra capacity is fitting fold-specific noise. Binning all 64,420 triplets by the color gap between the two candidates leaves the gain flat, 3.9 pp where color cannot discriminate against 4.9 pp where it separates them, so the residual is not a low-level effect.

What stops us certifying it is that a scalar label does not record which attribute a voter weighted, so a model trained on these majorities learns one weighting drawn from a population that does not agree on it. The JND labels show the same, disagreeing with 2AFC on 51.6% of the 608 stimuli that carry both (Fig3_rebuttal). The 4.2 pp is signal, but NIGHTS cannot show it is better human alignment, and separating the two needs the per-rater labels the protocol above collects.


### Generalization to other perceptual similarity datasets

The claim generalizes to BAPPS when each benchmark is measured against its own reproducibility floor, but not when the comparison is made per distortion category. Against the BAPPS floor of 69.0% and the NIGHTS floor of 70.1%, two methods clear both, DreamSim at 73.6 and 80.7 and a frozen DINOv1 at 71.5 and 74.1. BAPPS is real photographs with five human votes per triplet, and there LPIPS beats every zero-shot DINO backbone on color, deblur and super-resolution, so plain cosine is not competitive for low-level distortion detection. BAPPS also appears nowhere in our abstract and has no main-body table, despite being our only external validation, so we will move it into the main text.


### Less concrete guidance on a usable replacement protocol

Section 5 ends with a six-point specification, which we will move into its own subsection. One similarity axis varies per triplet, controlled at construction time, and the 2AFC question names that axis, so voters are not asked a generic "more similar". The release includes per-rater raw votes with anonymized identifiers and a recruitment summary. The vote count clears a target reproducibility floor, for which Section 4.3 gives the closed form. Fixed splits, tie-handling rules and a held-out distortion suite are distributed with the data.
